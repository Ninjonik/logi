# Matches and competitions

## Public match pages

Public matches are records with imported match statistics, not a live list of
all planned events. An event becomes eligible only when it is a non-training
match and points to its imported match-stat record. This intentionally excludes
draft plans, ordinary events, trainings, and partially imported results.

Open a public match to see its limited statistical projection: participating
sides, time, map information, and results when recorded. A match result can be
imported before a manager has recorded the community-facing outcome, so read
the result and event status separately.

Open a public match to see the information its community has made available: participating sides, planned or completed time, map information, and results when recorded. A match without a result is not necessarily cancelled—it may simply not have been concluded yet.

## Competitions

Standings are calculated from final and forfeit fixtures that have both scores.
For the ECL-style table, cap score is the first ranking value, then regular
wins, total wins, fewer total matches, and team name. Forfeits count as total
matches/wins where their score says so, but not as regular matches/wins. A
scheduled fixture or a fixture without both scores does not change standings.

The public competitions area lists every published competition. Competitions
that Logi's global administrators are still preparing are unpublished and do
not appear on these pages or in the public API. Every competition belongs to
one game (Hell Let Loose or Wardogs), and only matches for that game can be
linked to it.

Open a competition to see its game, how many teams play in how many
divisions, and the official rules and website when Logi knows them. Below,
pick a division in the tabs (the address keeps the choice, so a link opens the
same division). When the competition has play-off fixtures, a **Play-off** tab
gathers them from every division; league and relegation fixtures stay in their
division.

The **Table** lists each team with its **Played** (total matches), **Won**
(total wins) and **Points** (cap score). Teams are shown with their logo from
Logi's team catalogue, or with their short code or initials when they have no
logo; a withdrawn team is marked as such, and a team that represents a public
Logi clan links to that clan's page.

Beside the table, fixtures are grouped by round when the competition records
rounds ("Round 3 · this weekend", "Round 2 · results"), and by week when it does
not. The next round and the last played round are shown; **More rounds** opens
the others. Relegation and forfeit fixtures are labelled, and **Statistics**
opens the linked Logi match when one exists. Competition rules are owned by the
competition organiser; use the official rules link when one is provided.

<Steps>

### Find a match

Use the Community page, a clan page, or a competition fixture to open a public match.

### Check the state

Read the date, event status, and result separately. Each answers a different question: when it is/was scheduled, where it is in the workflow, and whether a score has been recorded.

### Need to change the match?

Only authorised server managers can edit the underlying event in their workspace dashboard.

</Steps>

## Wardogs match reports

Wardogs result cards and player match pages link to a shared public match
report. It shows assigned teams and factions, final scores, faction combat
and money graphs, and a searchable player table. Linked players open their
personal match statistics. K/D always has two decimal places; missing provider
metrics are shown as unknown. The displayed date is the match end time.
Actual score timelines appear when collected, so older games can show the
other graphs without a timeline. Discord previews use Wardogs kills, deaths
and money rather than HLL effectiveness fields.

## Clan team enrollment

Clan administrators can open **Competitions** in their clan sidebar to enroll
the team connected to their clan. **Your competitions** shows current entries;
**Join a competition** offers other suitable competitions, and withdrawn or
rejected entries stay in history. Use **Team profile** to create or connect a
team and edit its branding directly.
Teams from another clan cannot be enrolled or withdrawn.

A competition's Logi administrator chooses one registration policy:

- **Closed**: no new enrollments or re-enrollments.
- **Requires administrator approval**: a request waits for a Logi administrator
  to approve or reject it in the competition's Teams tab.
- **Automatic registration**: eligible teams are accepted immediately.

Existing competitions start closed to clan enrollment. Unpublished competitions
are unavailable for signup. Organisers assign accepted teams to divisions;
requesting enrollment does not let a clan select or change its division.
Pending and rejected requests do not enter standings or fixture selection.

A competition can require a minimum number of distinct clan members who have
signed into Logi, such as 49 for HLL or 30 for Wardogs. Leave the minimum blank
for no requirement. Imported profiles and bot-only player records do not count;
Steam verification and use of other Logi features are not required. The count is
checked on enrollment and re-enrollment, and dropping below it later does not
automatically withdraw a team. Older accounts without retained login evidence
need to sign in again to establish that evidence.

**Withdraw** keeps the registration, division, fixtures and results. It remains
available when signup closes. Signing up again follows the current registration
policy and minimum. Logi administrators can still organise catalogue teams that
do not use Logi, independently of clan self-service signup.

## League directory and basic teams

Open **Leagues** in the public navigation to browse all published Logi leagues,
filter by game, and search by name or season. Each league links to its divisions,
standings, fixtures and rules. The registration badge distinguishes closed signup,
automatic acceptance and applications requiring organiser approval.

**Register team** opens the account registration page for that league. Sign in
with Discord to identify your account; a Discord server, bot installation and
full clan workspace are not required. **Create team and register** uses the
competition game and immediately attempts registration. If too few players
have joined, the team is saved but registration remains incomplete; invite
players and register that existing team later. Under **My league teams**, you
can also create a standalone profile with its game, name and optional short
code and description. An account can
own up to five basic profiles. Existing catalogue names cannot be claimed by
creating a duplicate; ask a Logi organiser to link an existing catalogue team
through its clan if it already represents your community. Basic account profiles
cannot be merged through catalogue administration: merging must not silently
transfer ownership or invalidate player membership.

The same team can register for multiple competitions of its game. Each profile
lists its competition entries and links to their registration and fixtures.
**Edit profile** changes the name, short code, description and avatar without
a bot or organiser approval; owners and linked-clan administrators can edit.
Existing match snapshots keep their recorded branding.

Teams linked to clans you currently administer appear alongside basic profiles.
Owners can connect an existing basic team to a workspace they administer from
**Discord connection** inside the team profile, dashboard entry, or the
workspace **Team profile** page. Each Discord
server can link to one team across games. The team keeps its invitations, players,
registrations and fixtures; invited players and signed-in clan members count
once each. This does not grant players clan workspace access. Clan
**Competitions** and public league registration manage the same team enrollment.

Only a team owner or current clan administrator can register, withdraw, choose a
fixture server or report scores. Team players do not gain management permission.
Use **Join a team** to enter an invitation link or code from your team owner.
Opening an invitation puts joining first: sign in on the public site, then
explicitly choose **Join team**. You stay outside the dashboard. The public
account menu shows your name and avatar and links back to **My league teams**.
Basic-team owners can copy a private player invitation. Anyone with the invitation
can sign in and explicitly join; share it only with your players. Each account
counts once toward a league's minimum, and joining twice does not add another
member. Clan-linked teams count distinct invited players and signed-in clan
members without counting a person twice. Admission and division assignment follow the organiser's existing
policy. Invitations and pending reports are not public.

### Fixture hosting and results

The league registration page includes fixtures for your teams; Logi organisers
can manage all fixtures in the selected league. Before there are linked events,
score reports or a final result, choose the match server:

- **Connected Logi server:** select a connection from a clan you administer. It
  must be enabled, online, error-free, support match history, and have a successful
  observation within the last 15 minutes. This does not require a Discord bot for
  the basic team. If another clan supplies the server, its administrator selects
  the connection. Normal match imports and organiser event-linking supply the
  recorded match statistics; choosing a server does not create a match event.
- **External server:** enter its display name. Only manual scores are supported;
  no statistics or match-event links are imported for this fixture. Names are
  labels, never provider URLs, passwords or credentials.

For an external fixture, each team owner reports the scores in the displayed team
order. Scores affect standings only when all participants submit the same values,
or a Logi organiser approves one report. Conflicting reports remain pending. If the organiser changes the participating
teams or their order, old reports must be submitted again and cannot be approved
for the new pairing.
Two-team and three-team fixtures are supported. Final results lock further team
reports; organisers can correct a final score using competition administration.
Changing hosting after reports, event links or results is blocked so an existing
result cannot silently be moved to another server.

The public league pages show the server type and label, never connection IDs,
provider addresses, invitations or provisional score reports. Basic-team actions
are available through the personal session API at `/api/v1/me/leagues`; clan
service keys cannot grant account ownership. The public league catalogue is
available at `/api/v1/public/leagues` and league details keep the existing
`/api/v1/public/competitions/{slug}` contract.
