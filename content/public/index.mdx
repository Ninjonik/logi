# For visitors and players

The public Logi site introduces the clan tools and lets you explore participating communities, matches and competitions. You do not need to sign in to browse it.

## See how clan management works

The [Logi home page](/en) walks through setup and the weekly event workflow:
plan a match, collect sign-ups in Discord and publish a roster. Scroll through
three steps while the example stays in view; scrolling back reverses the steps.
The current step's name and number appear above a progress bar, with small tick
marks at the two points where the step changes. Steps switch
without fades, and the layout stays fixed. Each preview fits in its frame without
internal scrolling or panning. Logi and all its features are free. The page also
covers attendance reminders, match history, trainings, members, competitions,
integrations, articles and self-hosting. Roster updates reach Discord when you
publish; automatic DMs to affected players depend on the change-notification
setting in the publish dialog. Draft edits remain private.

Dashboard screenshots show the Valkyria community and have matching light and
dark versions. Select a screenshot to enlarge it. They are in English; page copy
and the example Discord messages follow the selected English, Czech or German
language. Discord examples use fictional data and do not send messages or change
any clan records. The **Get started** and **Open app** actions lead to sign-in or
your existing dashboard; [getting started](/wiki/getting-started) covers setup.

## What is public?

Public pages intentionally expose a limited projection of Logi data. They may
show community identity, active-member counts, recorded match history, player
match statistics, and competition fixtures. They do not expose Discord IDs,
platform IDs, member notes, assignments, unpublished rosters, private Discord
configuration, or management controls.

Public visibility is currently data-driven rather than a per-page publishing
switch. A clan can appear once it has at least one active member (or a recorded
member ID), and a player profile appears only after qualifying recorded match
statistics exist. A planned match is not public merely because it was created:
it appears on its clan's page only once it has been announced (its announcement
time has passed), is not a draft and is a match rather than a training, and
then only with its time, game, opponent and category.

The exact information on an eligible page depends on the records Logi has for
that player, clan, match, or competition.

<Callout type="warning">
    A public profile or match page is a view, not a management console. Sign in
    and open the dashboard when you need to change server information.
</Callout>
